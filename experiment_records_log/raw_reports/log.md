Methodological Note
For low-velocity obstacle-avoidance, a pure CLF-CBF feasibility formulation is not sufficient because the CLF control authority can become weak or ambiguous near zero velocity, allowing the optimizer to prefer small-norm actions that are safe but non-progressive. A hybrid cost-and-constraint formulation is therefore necessary: the CLF and CBF remain as explicit safety/stability constraints, while the quadratic objective is biased toward a goal-directed nominal control u_nom. This preserves formal constraint handling while supplying a persistent directional prior, improving robustness against stagnation and retreating behaviors in near-hover regimes.

Paper Insight
A pure CLF-CBF-QP can become geometrically under-informative near hover because the CLF authority weakens at low velocity, and the cheapest feasible action in a nearly collinear obstacle-goal configuration may be to retreat rather than to route around. Our obstacle-aware hybrid formulation resolves this by augmenting the nominal control with a local tangential flow-field term around nearby obstacles. The QP still enforces CLF/CBF constraints, but its objective is no longer merely “move toward the goal if possible”; it becomes “move toward the goal with an explicit circumvention preference.” This preserves safety while injecting directional structure exactly where the low-velocity CLF loses practical guidance.

A pure CLF-CBF-QP becomes geometrically under-informative near hover because, at low velocity, the CLF input Lie derivative loses effective directional authority: the CLF constraint still bounds admissible accelerations, but it no longer supplies a strong preference for a specific circumvention direction. In a nearly collinear goal-obstacle configuration, the minimum-effort feasible solution can therefore be retreating rather than routing around the obstacle. We address this with a principled hybrid formulation: the CLF and CBF remain explicit constraints, while the nominal acceleration in the quadratic objective is upgraded to an obstacle-aware flow-field command composed of a goal-directed term plus a locally activated tangential component around nearby forward obstacles. This preserves safety filtering, restores directional structure in the low-velocity regime, and ensures that the tangential bias washes out after the obstacle is no longer ahead of the vehicle.
